Community Guidelines

Last updated: 10 October 2026

Loopinn only works when people feel safe saying hello. These guidelines describe how we expect everyone to behave, how to stay safe when you meet someone, and what happens when someone crosses the line. They are part of our Terms of Use.

Our four rules

  • Be real. Real name, real photos of you, real age. You must be 18 or older.
  • Be kind. Treat people the way you'd want to be treated by a stranger.
  • Respect “no”. A declined Loop, an ignored message or a block is the end of the conversation.
  • Keep it safe. Nothing that hurts, exploits, scams or endangers anyone.

What isn't allowed

Loopinn has zero tolerance for objectionable content and abusive users. Content and accounts that break these rules are removed, and people who break them lose access to Loopinn.

Involve minorsUsing Loopinn under 18; any sexual or exploitative content involving a child; trying to contact a minor. See Child Safety Standards.
Harass or threatenBullying, intimidation, stalking, repeated unwanted contact after being declined or blocked, threats of any kind.

Abuse the serviceBots, scraping, spam, malware, evading blocks, rate limits or age checks.

Profile and photo standards

  • Your first photo should clearly show your face. Every photo should be of you, taken by you or with permission.
  • No nudity or sexually suggestive photos, no children in photos without a parent's consent, and no photos of other people who haven't agreed.
  • No logos, contact details, social handles or ads in your bio or photos.
  • Say honestly what you're open to — dating, friends or hangouts — and respect what others say they're open to.

Loops and chat etiquette

  • A Loop is an invitation, not an obligation. If someone doesn't accept, let it go.
  • Chat opens only when both of you say yes — keep it respectful and consensual.
  • If a conversation stops feeling good, you can mute it, block the person, or report them at any time.

Safety tips for meeting people

Before you meet

  • Chat first. A video call or a few messages can help you feel at ease.
  • Keep personal details — your home or work address, workplace, financial details — private until you really trust someone.
  • Be wary of anyone who asks for money, rushes you, or asks you to move to another app straight away. These are common scam signs.
  • Use Ghost Mode whenever you'd prefer not to be seen nearby.

When you meet

  • Meet in a busy public place and stay in public for the first few meetings.
  • Tell a friend who you are meeting, where, and when you expect to be back. Keep your phone charged.
  • Arrange your own transport to and from the meeting.
  • Stay sober enough to make good decisions, and never leave drinks unattended.
  • Trust your instincts. You can leave at any time, for any reason.

If something feels wrong

  • In an emergency, contact your local emergency services immediately (for example 112, 911 or 999).
  • Block and report the person in Loopinn so we can act, even if you aren't sure.

How to report or block someone

  1. Open the person's profile or your chat with them.
  2. Tap the ⋯ button (in a chat, open the menu and choose Report or block).
  3. Choose Report and pick a reason — harassment, fake profile, inappropriate content, spam or scam, seems under 18, safety concern, or something else — and add details if you can. Or choose Block.

Blocking is instant and mutual: neither of you can see the other again and any chat ends. You can review and undo blocks under Me → Blocked people. You can also email hello@loopinn.me about anything you can't report in the app.

What happens after you report

  • We review every report. We aim to look at reports of serious harm — threats, child safety, impersonation — within 24 hours, and the rest as quickly as we can.
  • Reports are confidential. We never tell the person that you reported them.
  • Depending on what we find we may remove content, warn the person, limit features, suspend or permanently remove the account, and, when required by law or when someone may be in danger, contact the authorities.
  • If you think we got a decision wrong, you can ask us to review it. See Reporting, moderation and enforcement.
